## Interpretation and limits

The gates remain 30 seconds for ordinary locations, 180 seconds for polar/sensitive locations, 120 seconds for seasonal timestamps, and any daylight-state disagreement. These are investigation thresholds, not achieved accuracy guarantees. Every rounded-minute disagreement is retained even when below a gate.

A confirmed grazing example is 2000-12-21 at 66.56°N, 180°W: the JPL-based reference gives 86.25 seconds of geometric daylight while the browser model gives zero. The reference maximum altitude is slightly above the horizon; the model maximum is slightly below it, differing by about 0.55 arcseconds at the reference peak. Small angular errors can therefore change the daylight state.

The separate full-year 2026 breakdown covers 41,975 cases. Its largest ordinary-location difference is **0.382 seconds** and its largest polar difference is **108.715 seconds (1 minute 49 seconds)**. The polar maximum occurs at 66.56°N, 90°W on 2026-12-21: the reference gives 108.715 seconds of daylight while the browser gives zero. See the [2026 breakdown](2026-10-30-current-year.json).

The full grid’s worst case is **1,116.914 seconds (18 minutes 37 seconds)** on 2100-03-18 at 89°S, 0°. The individual-day oracle agrees with the batched oracle within one microsecond. The browser predicts an additional short dark interval near midnight, while the DE440s-based reference remains just above the horizon then. This is a polar grazing case: the first-hour minimum is about +0.00000765° in the reference and -0.00076588° in the browser model. See the [outlier diagnostic](2026-10-30-outlier-check.json).

The largest seasonal timestamp difference in the full run is 148.794 seconds. Year-2100 differences include substantial contributions from different time conversions and future Earth-rotation estimates, as well as orbital-model differences. Historical UTC labels and future leap seconds/Earth rotation have additional uncertainty. These clock differences should not be interpreted purely as solar orbital-position error.

Both calculations use the solar center, an airless 0° horizon, WGS84 observers at zero elevation, and a fixed 24-hour local mean solar date. No atmospheric refraction, solar-disc radius, terrain or weather is included. This is agreement with an independent numerical reference, not field observations or conventional published sunrise/sunset times. Polar cases are deliberately oversampled; the combined percentiles are not population-weighted global accuracy estimates.

The uploaded file contains an internal JPL DE440 identification and publication citation. The user reported a direct official download; this environment checked the kernel and recorded its SHA-256, but did not independently retrieve it from JPL. All tools, kernel data, fixtures and reports stay outside the website runtime. Production JS and CSS remain unchanged.

Next accuracy work should address polar grazing sensitivity and explicitly reconcile time conventions before making a universal minute-accuracy claim.
